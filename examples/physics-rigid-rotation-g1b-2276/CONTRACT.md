## Candidate choice: eighth-order composition of coupled midpoint/Cayley maps

A single second-order midpoint step has a phase error inconsistent with the retained tight trajectory target. Use a fixed eighth-order symmetric composition; do not widen the target. This is a numerical flow, not a claim of exact integration.

Let l be body angular momentum, D=diag(1/I), and R map body to world. The torque-free equations are ldot=l cross D*l and Rdot=R*[D*l]cross. At each signed substep s, solve the implicit midpoint equation

`l1-l0 = s*(lm cross D*lm), lm=(l0+l1)/2`.

Then `R1=R0*Cayley(s*[D*lm]cross)`. Since `(I+sW/2)l1=(I-sW/2)l0`, this same Cayley map sends l1 to l0: world R*l is preserved. Midpoint preserves the two quadratic forms l·l and l·D*l; hence energy and momentum magnitude. Orthogonal Cayley preserves proper rotation. The quaternion increment is normalized `[s*omega_mid/2,1]`, right-multiplied in body frame, preserving the old XYZW/body-to-world convention. Return world omega=R1*D*l1. Reconstruct body momentum from the actual current q/world omega at each macrostep; do not create a hidden momentum state or use initial l as a later truth cache.

Compose S2 into S4/S6/S8 using `ak=1/(2-2^(1/k)), bk=1-2*ak`, k=3,5,7, and `Snext(h)=Sprev(ak*h) Sprev(bk*h) Sprev(ak*h)`. Apply the resulting27 signed midpoint maps in frozen recursive order.
