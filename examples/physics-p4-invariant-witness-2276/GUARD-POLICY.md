## Entry proof and loop invariant

GCD actually calls the original checked operation on BOTH entry operands before entering the loop. This performs their charged BIT_LENGTH inspections and16385-bit checks and records their exact magnitude peak. The original charged sign comparisons and checked NEG operation normalize them to nonnegative values. Only after complete admission does guardedGcdEntries increment.

Let M denote the mathematical maximum of the two absolute entry values. This is a proof symbol, not an unmetered runtime maximum calculation. At loop entry, `0<=a<=M` and `0<=b<=M`. Each iteration reserves CMP1 before the actual `b===0n` comparison. If zero, return a. Otherwise b is positive by the invariant. Reserve REM1 BEFORE the actual BigInt `a%b`. Nonnegative BigInt dividend and positive divisor imply `0<=r<b<=M`. Reassigning `(a,b)=(b,r)` preserves the entry bound and strictly decreases nonzero b. GCD is preserved by the Euclidean identity. This also covers an initially smaller a, equal values, signed inputs after normalization and gcd(0,0)=0.

Every remainder's bit length is at most that of an entry value already actually measured and recorded. It cannot increase the exact intermediate peak or exceed the retained integer limit. The pre-allocation bound therefore exists before REM; it follows from an established invariant rather than a new operand inspection. No repeated BIT_LENGTH, zero-divisor test, sign test or result range comparison is performed inside the loop, so no such operation is priced.
